Ecocare Kertih · Mangrove Guided Tour

Ecocare Mangrove Trail: Key to the 20 Plants

A guide for identifying the 18 trail plants and the 2 "Around Ecocare" trees without flowers, using growth form, roots, bark, leaves and propagules. Two pairs of look-alikes (the two bakau, and Berus and Tumu Merah) are told apart by their propagules. Scientific terms are given in bold, with a plain explanation in brackets the first time they appear. All terms are also in the Glossary. Every fact has a source in brackets, listed in full at the end.

The guide has two tools:

  • The key: answer a series of "a or b?" questions until you reach a name. Best for beginners and kids.
  • The character matrix: two tables listing the features of all 20 plants. Start from whatever you can see, and rule out plants that don't match. Best when a feature in the key is hard to see.

Status: draft, not yet tested on the trail (checked against sources 29 Oct 2026).

How to use the key

  1. Start at couplet 1. Each couplet has two choices, a and b.
  2. Read both choices before deciding. Pick the one that fits your plant better, then follow the arrow to the next couplet or to a name.
  3. Most choices list two or three features. The first one is the most reliable. If it is hard to see, use the others.
  4. Look at several leaves, not just one. Shade, damage and age can change leaf size and shape.
  5. Use grown trees. Young saplings may not have their special roots yet (Nyireh Bunga's buttresses, for example, are usually not seen on young trees (NParks)).
  6. When you reach a name, go to Check your answer and confirm it with a second feature, such as the fruit or propagule.

Handy size guide: a new pencil is about 18 cm long, and an adult's palm is about 8 cm wide.

Leaf or leaflet? Look where it joins the twig. A whole leaf has a small bud (a tiny shoot) in the angle above its stalk. A leaflet (one part of a divided leaf) never has a bud at its base.

Safety first

  • Buta-buta: never break its leaves or twigs. The milky sap irritates skin, and in the eyes it can cause painful inflammation and, in severe cases, blindness (Atlas of Poisonous Plants in Hong Kong).
  • Pandan laut: the leaf edges have sharp spines. Don't run your hand along a leaf.
  • Don't step on breathing roots, and don't pull propagules off the trees.
  • Nyireh bunga: don't stand under a tree with ripe fruit. The woody fruits are the size of a grapefruit or small pomelo (NParks).
  • Ketapang: biting ants can live in its hollow twigs, so don't break them.

The key

1
1a

A woody climber (a vine with a woody stem) with pinnate leaves (one leaf made of several leaflets along a central stalk): 3 to 7 leathery leaflets, ending in a single leaflet that is slightly bigger than the others. Leaves alternate. It grows 15 m long or more, twining over other plants or creeping on the ground (NParks).

→ Tuba Laut (Derris trifoliata)

1b

Not a woody climber with pinnate leaves.

→ go to 2

2
2a

Palm-like plant. Leaves very long (more than 60 cm), strap-shaped or feather-shaped, with parallel veins (veins running side by side from base to tip, like in grass).

→ go to 3

2b

Not palm-like. Leaves (or leaf-like parts) less than 40 cm long. The largest, Ketapang's, reach about 38 cm (PROSEA).

→ go to 4

3
3a

No upright trunk. The stem creeps underground as a rhizome. Huge feather-shaped fronds, 4 to 10 m long, rise straight out of the mud, each with up to 100 long leaflets on each side (NParks).

→ Nipah (Nypa fruticans)

3b

A branching trunk, up to 14 m tall, with prop and stilt roots (roots that grow out of the lower trunk and branches and down into the ground). Leaves strap-shaped (long and narrow, like a belt), 80 to 180 cm long and 4 to 8 cm wide, in a spiral at the branch tips, with spines along the edges and the underside of the midrib (NParks).

→ Pandan Laut (Pandanus tectorius)

4
4a

Leaves pinnate, with leaflets 3.5 to 12 cm long with rounded tips. Bark smooth and flaky, light brown, yellowish or greenish, or grey with light brown to orange patches. Mature trees have long buttresses that snake along the ground (NParks).

→ Nyireh Bunga (Xylocarpus granatum)

4b

Leaves simple (one undivided blade on each leaf stalk, with a bud at its base).

→ go to 5

5
5a

Phyllodes (what look like leaves are really flattened leaf stalks): narrow and falcate (curved like a sickle), 10 to 20 cm long and 1.2 to 3 cm wide, with 3 lengthwise nerves running side by side (WATTLE).

→ Akasia (Acacia auriculiformis)

5b

True leaves with a midrib (a main central vein) and side veins branching off it.

→ go to 6

6
6a

Leaves opposite (in pairs: two leaves at the same point on the twig, facing each other). In the mangrove family Rhizophoraceae the pairs are decussate (each pair at right angles to the pair below) (Hou 1958).

→ go to 7

6b

Leaves alternate (one leaf at each point, taking turns up the twig; in some, in a spiral).

→ go to 16

7
7a

Not a tree: an upright bush up to about 3 m tall, or a climber up to 13 m long. Leaves thin, 3.8 to 8.8 cm long (NParks), with tiny glands dotted on the underside (CSIRO).

→ Gambir Laut (Clerodendrum inerme, syn. Volkameria inermis)

7b

A tree.

→ go to 8

8
8a

No ring scars on the twigs. Pneumatophores (breathing roots that grow straight up out of the mud around the tree) (NParks; NParks).

→ go to 9

8b

Ring scars around the twigs at each pair of leaves. They are left by interpetiolar stipules (a pointed sheath between the two leaf stalks that wraps the shoot tip and falls off early) (Hou 1958). This is the mangrove family Rhizophoraceae. The trees have stilt roots, knee roots, or no special roots.

→ go to 11

9
9a

Pneumatophores thick, blunt and cone-shaped, up to 1 m tall. Leaves 5 to 11 cm long and 4 to 8 cm wide, with a rounded or broad tip (NParks).

→ Perepat (Sonneratia alba)

9b

Pneumatophores thin like pencils (akar pensel), 15 to 20 cm tall in Api-api Putih (NParks; NParks). Api-api Putih's leaves have salt glands that push out salt (NParks).

→ go to 10

10
10a

Leaves narrow and pointed: oblong to lanceolate (narrow and tapering), up to 15 cm long and 5 cm wide. Underside whitish (NParks).

→ Api-api Putih (Avicennia alba)

10b

Leaves broad: ovate (egg-shaped) or elliptic (oval), up to 10 cm long and 6 cm wide, thick. Underside covered in dense, powdery hairs (NParks).

→ Api-api Bulu (Avicennia rumphiana)

11
11a

Stilt roots (akar jangkang: arching roots that grow out of the trunk and down into the mud, like spider legs).

→ go to 12

11b

No stilt roots.

→ go to 14

12
12a

Stilt roots small, only at the base of the trunk. Leaf tip blunt (NParks).

→ Tengar Merah (Ceriops zippeliana)

12b

Stilt roots large and arching, in Bakau Minyak reaching up to 5 m up the stem (NParks). Leaf tip pointed, ending in a short stiff point (a mucro) (Ngernsaengsaruay et al. 2024).

→ go to 13

13
13a

On propagules still hanging on the tree, the collar (the short tube at the top, where the propagule comes out of the fruit) is pale greenish: pale green or greenish yellow (Ngernsaengsaruay et al. 2024).

→ Bakau Kurap (Rhizophora mucronata)

13b

On propagules still hanging on the tree, the collar is reddish: red, dark greenish red or reddish dark green (Ngernsaengsaruay et al. 2024).

→ Bakau Minyak (Rhizophora apiculata)

No propagules on the tree? Come back in another season.

14
14a

Knee roots (akar lutut: roots that loop up out of the mud and back down, like bent knees) (NParks; NParks).

→ go to 15

14b

No knee roots. Leaf edges curled inward. Trunk base thickened, and the roots sometimes look braided (NParks).

→ Bakau Lilin (Kandelia candel)

15
15a

Propagules thin, 8 to 15 cm long and 0.8 cm wide (NParks).

→ Berus (Bruguiera cylindrica)

15b

Propagules fat and cigar-shaped, 19 to 25 cm long and 1 to 1.7 cm wide (NParks).

→ Tumu Merah (Bruguiera gymnorhiza)

No propagules on the tree? Look for fallen ones on the ground underneath.

16
16a

Leaves cordate (heart-shaped, with a notch where the stalk joins), with a pointed tip and finely toothed edge, spirally arranged (NParks), the blade 5 to 30 cm long. Underside whitish and densely covered in stellate hairs (tiny star-shaped hairs, visible with a hand lens) (Plants of SE NSW).

→ Bebaru (Hibiscus tiliaceus, syn. Talipariti tiliaceum)

16b

Leaves not heart-shaped.

→ go to 17

17
17a

Leaf underside scaly, silvery white to brown, shining like a mirror. Leaves 6.3 to 30 cm long, spirally arranged. Plank or ribbon buttresses at the trunk base (NParks).

→ Dungun Laut (Heritiera littoralis)

17b

Leaf underside not silvery-scaly.

→ go to 18

18
18a

Leaves large, usually 15 to 25 cm long (up to 38 cm), obovate, shiny, clustered at the branch tips. Branches horizontal, in tiers 1 to 2 m apart, giving young trees a pagoda shape (PROSEA).

→ Ketapang (Terminalia catappa)

18b

Leaves small, up to 11 cm long (NParks; NParks).

→ go to 19

19
19a

Leaf edge smooth (not toothed). Leaves obovate (egg-shaped, widest near the tip), 2 to 9 cm long and 1 to 2.5 cm wide, slightly thick and fleshy, with a rounded or retuse tip (shallowly notched) (NParks), spirally arranged in tufts near the twig ends (PROSEA).

→ Teruntum Merah (Lumnitzera littorea)

19b

Leaf edge toothed: shallow teeth, with 2 to 4 tiny glands on each side where the blade meets the leaf stalk. Leaves oval to drop-shaped, 3.7 to 11 cm long, spirally arranged. Old leaves turn bright red (NParks). Don't touch or break any part of this tree.

→ Buta-buta (Excoecaria agallocha)

Once you have a name, confirm it with one of these. Fruits and propagules are seasonal, but old ones often lie on the ground under the tree.

Propagule tip: roll it between your fingers. Tengar Merah has angular ridges (bersegi) (NParks), Berus is thin and round (NParks), and Tumu Merah is fat like a cigar (NParks).

Limits of this guide

  • It only covers these 20 plants. A plant that isn't on the list (for example Acacia mangium, Teruntum Putih, Berembang or Thespesia populnea) will still end up at a name, so it can be misidentified. If nothing in "Check your answer" matches, it may be a different species.
  • Seasonal couplets: couplets 13 and 15 need propagules.
  • Young trees: couplets 8 to 14 rely on roots. Saplings may not have their stilt roots or knee roots yet.
  • Leaf sizes are typical ranges, not strict limits. Look at several leaves.

Glossary

Term Plain meaning Malay (where used at Ecocare)
Alternate leaves One leaf at each point on the twig, taking turns up the stem
Apiculate Ending in a small, short point
Bud A tiny undeveloped shoot; whole leaves have one at the base of their stalk, leaflets don't tunas
Buttress A flared or wing-like base of the trunk that helps support the tree akar banir
Calyx The ring of outer flower parts (sepals); in many mangroves it stays on the fruit or propagule
Collar The short tube at the top of a Rhizophora propagule, where it comes out of the fruit
Cordate Heart-shaped, with a notch where the stalk joins bentuk hati
Cork warts Tiny black dots on the leaf underside of Rhizophora bintik hitam
Decussate Opposite pairs of leaves, each pair at right angles to the next
Elliptic Oval, widest in the middle
Entire A smooth leaf edge, with no teeth or lobes
Falcate Curved like a sickle
Gland A small bump that produces a substance
Interpetiolar stipules A pointed sheath between the stalks of a pair of opposite leaves, which falls off early and leaves a ring scar
Knee roots Roots that loop up out of the mud and back down, like bent knees akar lutut
Lanceolate Narrow and tapering to a point, like a spearhead
Leaflet One of the small blades that make up a pinnate leaf; it has no bud at its base anak daun
Lenticels Small raised pores on bark or roots that let air in
Midrib The main vein running down the middle of a leaf
Mucro A short, sharp point at the leaf tip
Mucronate Ending in a short, stiff point
Obovate Egg-shaped, widest near the tip
Opposite leaves Two leaves at the same point on the twig, facing each other berpasangan
Ovate Egg-shaped, widest near the base
Parallel veins Veins running side by side from base to tip
Petiole Leaf stalk tangkai daun
Phyllode A flattened leaf stalk that looks and works like a leaf
Pinnate One leaf made of several leaflets along a central stalk, like a feather daun majmuk
Plank buttress A flat, board-like root that winds along the ground
Pneumatophore A breathing root that grows up out of the mud akar pensel (thin ones)
Prop roots Roots that drop from the branches down towards the ground, like the long hanging root on the big Bakau Minyak. Some sources use "prop root" loosely for stilt roots too
Propagule A seedling that has already started growing on the parent tree (in viviparous mangroves) anak benih, "buah"
Retuse Rounded with a shallow notch at the tip
Rhizome A stem that grows horizontally under the ground or mud rizom
Ring scar A ring-shaped mark left on the twig where a stipule fell off
Salt glands Tiny glands on the leaf that push out excess salt
Simple leaf A leaf with one undivided blade daun tunggal
Stellate hairs Tiny star-shaped hairs
Stilt roots Arching roots growing from the lower trunk into the mud, like spider legs (Rhizophora, Tengar Merah, Pandan Laut) akar jangkang
